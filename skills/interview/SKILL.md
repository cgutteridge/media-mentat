# Taste interview

Read the [main skill](../../SKILL.md), then the private record's constraints,
clusters and relevant session answers. Keep the interview grounded in works the
user has confirmed experiencing. Ask **one question at a time**, listen for the
reason, record the answer, and use it to choose the next question.

## Questions that distinguish explanations

Alternate open questions and comparisons where they resolve an actual uncertainty:

- “What do you like about this one?” Follow vague labels with a concrete aspect
  such as voice, character relationships, ideas, setting, structure or emotional effect.
- “A or B tonight—and why?” Accept both, neither, depends, or different reasons.
  Familiar works make safer comparisons than hypothetical unfamiliar pitches.
- “You disliked that feature elsewhere; what made it work here?” Learn exceptions
  and whether an objection is a firm exclusion or a contextual preference.
- “Does that change when you listen rather than read?” Explore medium, mood,
  effort and commitment where those affect the request.

Avoid supplying a flattering interpretation for the user to agree with. Keep
unread works Unread-safe even while testing a hypothesis. A user can explain an
effect without describing a plot; ask about spoiler permission if a comparison
would expose an unconfirmed work.

## Record several clusters

Use the record's Taste clusters section. For each cluster capture:

- User's own label or a clearly provisional agent label.
- Confirmed anchor works and **their stated reasons** for liking them.
- Conditions: mood, medium, desired intensity, length or social context.
- Counterexamples, boundaries and exceptions.
- Agent hypotheses with confidence and a useful next question, separate from facts.

Clusters may overlap, coexist or remain unrelated. Do not force contradictory
preferences into one average, rank all works on one axis, or infer enthusiasm for
everything between two favourites. Genre membership alone is not a taste reason.
Retain earlier answers with dates and context when preferences change; mark an
explicit correction as superseding the earlier statement.

## Stop and revisit

Stop the initial interview when there are enough stated reasons and constraints
to support the requested pass, or when the user wants recommendations. Explore
more than one cluster if they offer contrasting favourites, but never force a
second cluster or a complete questionnaire. Show a brief provisional understanding
and let the user correct it.

Revisit a **specific** gap after repeated misses, a newly mentioned kind of
enjoyment, changed circumstances, a conflicting hypothesis, or an explicit “grill
me”. Routine feedback usually needs only one follow-up, not a restarted interview.
Persist each answer in dated Session addenda and update relevant clusters and
constraints. Consumption confirmations also update the queryable certainty table.
