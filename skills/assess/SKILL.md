# Assess personal fit

Apply the [main skill](../../SKILL.md). This mode answers a user's question about
a supplied work; it does not need to generate a new shortlist or establish award
membership. It applies to fiction and nonfiction, subject to available evidence.

## Establish the question and evidence

Identify the exact work, creator and relevant version/medium. Clarify ambiguous
titles or adaptations before attaching warnings. Learn whether the user wants a
quick verdict, a comparison, a specific concern checked, or help deciding whether
the commitment is worthwhile. Do not turn every assessment into an interview.

Read the private constraints, relevant taste clusters, Consumption certainty and
previous feedback. Search aliases and creator notes. If taste evidence is missing,
ask one focused question; a general review is not a personalised assessment.
A friend's enthusiasm is a lead, not proof of the user's enjoyment.

Use [research](../research/SKILL.md) to establish relevant voice, pace, structure,
tone, subject treatment, content concerns or format demands. Award membership
is unnecessary for a user-supplied work: use source-linked private research notes
or Discoverable Facts. Apply the Evidence gate when reusing cache findings; an
unrelated empty Living List cache need not block direct research. Consumption
certainty still governs spoilers for every candidate and comparison work.

## Explain appeal and friction separately

Compare evidenced characteristics to the user's **stated reasons**, contextual
preferences and exclusions. A work can fit one cluster and clash with another.
Describe that tension rather than average it into a score or assume a single
continuum. Distinguish likely enjoyment, tolerance and willingness to experiment.

Check both likely appeal and possible turn-offs. Relevant friction might concern
style, pacing, repetition, emotional intensity, treatment of a subject, content,
series commitment or the particular edition/performance. These are examples to
investigate, not universal user preferences or a mandatory warning checklist.
Content warnings are one kind of personal warning; distinguish hard exclusions
from softer friction. A possible turn-off need not make a work a poor choice.

Tie each concern to a recorded preference and source evidence. Separate an
observed feature from the prediction about the user's reaction. State uncertainty
and conflicting evidence; avoid invented probabilities and guarantees. If a
specific concern cannot be verified, say it is **unverified**, not absent. A
source's silence does not establish that a work is safe for that concern.

## Deliver an Unread-safe assessment

Lead with a calibrated verdict such as likely fit, mixed fit, likely poor fit or
insufficient evidence. Follow with the reasons that actually help this decision:

- What is likely to appeal, connected to relevant taste clusters.
- What may grate or cross a stated boundary, with severity/context when evidenced.
- Confidence and what remains unknown, with supporting source links.
- A practical choice when supported: try a sample, choose a particular format,
  save it for a different mood, or pass. Sampling must be accessible and does not
  establish that later content avoids a concern.

Keep warnings at the broadest useful, Unread-safe level. A precise warning can
itself reveal a plot event. If answering the concern requires such a reveal and
Consumption certainty does not permit spoilers, offer a broad assessment and
explain that the detailed concern remains withheld. Confirm prior consumption
when relevant; never mark a work `won't-read` just to unlock a warning. Only the
established certainty rules permit plot details, including in warnings and in
questions about warnings. General interest in a warning grants no spoiler permission.

For “A or B?”, compare each work against relevant clusters and the user's current
context; both, neither and different moods remain valid outcomes. For an already
consumed work, discuss the recorded reaction when relevant rather than pretending
it is a fresh discovery. Offer alternatives only when requested or useful after
the supplied suggestion has been addressed.

## Remember

Store the supplied work, evidence references, verdict and open concerns in private
history. Record explicit new preferences or corrections in the taste record.
Receiving a suggestion is not liking it; asking about a warning does not establish
a permanent exclusion. Update consumption only from confirmed user statements.
The actual reaction after trying it can refine the relevant cluster later.
